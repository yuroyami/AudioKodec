# Decode G.722 and G.726

G.722 and G.726 are the ITU's ADPCM telephony codings: G.722 carries 7 kHz
wideband speech at 64 kbit/s, and G.726 narrowband speech at 16, 24, 32 or
40 kbit/s. Both turn up in call recordings and in WAV and AU files from
telephony equipment, and both decode through the same API as every other
format.

```kotlin
val info = AudioKodec.probe(wavBytes)
println(info.codec)   // G722 or G726

val samples = AudioKodec.decode(wavBytes).samples   // one channel
```

## Where they are read

| Container | G.722 | G.726 |
|---|---|---|
| WAV | Format tag 0x028F | Format tag 0x0045; the bits per sample, 2 to 5, give the rate |
| AU | Encoding 24 | Encodings 23 (Sun's G.721, 32 kbit/s), 25 and 26 (Sun's G.723 at 24 and 40 kbit/s) |

The order of code words inside a byte is not the same in the two
containers. In WAV, a byte's first code word starts at its most significant
bit, as ffmpeg writes and reads it; in AU, at its least significant bit, as
Sun's own G.721 and G.723 code packed it. Both codings are defined for one
channel, and a file that states more is refused.

## G.726

The decoder is written from section 4 of Recommendation G.726 (12/90), whose
sub-blocks define every step in integer arithmetic on fields of stated
widths. It decodes every rate to the reconstructed signal SR, the uniform
14-bit PCM that the Recommendation's decoder converts to A-law or mu-law, and
divides it by 8192. An overloaded stream can carry SR past that range, and
the Recommendation's 16-bit arithmetic can even wrap it; its own PCM stage
then saturates to the largest code word, and so does AudioKodec, so samples
stay within -1.0 to 1.0.

The Recommendation's Appendix II publishes test sequences for checking a
decoder: code words, and the A-law and mu-law output each must give, from
reset and after the homing sequences, for speech-like and overload inputs and
for code word sequences no encoder would emit. With the Recommendation's
output stage added on top, the decoder reproduces every output word of all
80 of them, at every rate.

### Files ffmpeg writes

ffmpeg's G.726 encoder and decoder, as of ffmpeg 6.1, do not follow the
Recommendation. Given the Appendix II code words, ffmpeg's decoder gives
samples 28 to 32 dB from the expected output at 16 to 32 kbit/s and 7 dB from
it at 40 kbit/s. AudioKodec's are 38 to 43 dB from it at every rate, the
distance of mu-law quantisation alone. ffmpeg's encoder models its own
decoder, so:

- at 16, 24 and 32 kbit/s, files ffmpeg writes decode here to samples close
  to ffmpeg's, but not equal to them;
- at 40 kbit/s, a file ffmpeg writes does not decode well with a decoder that
  follows the Recommendation, this one included. Files from conforming
  equipment decode correctly.

ffmpeg ends a stream after its last whole code word, part way through the
last block of eight at 24 and 40 kbit/s, and gives the length in the `fact`
chunk. Such a file decodes to the stated length.

## G.722

Both sub-band ADPCM decoders are written from sections 4 to 6 of
Recommendation G.722 (11/88), in its 16-bit arithmetic of saturating
additions and products shifted right by 15. The Recommendation's own test
sequences, from its Appendix II, check them with the receive QMF bypassed:
the lower sub-band in each of the three modes and the higher sub-band, for an
encoder's output on tones, d.c., noise and overflow and for an artificial
sequence that drives the predictors over their whole range. Every output
word matches.

The receive QMF is left partly open by the Recommendation, which allows any
accumulator of 24 bits or more and truncation or rounding, and its test
sequences do not cover it. AudioKodec accumulates exactly, truncates, and
keeps one bit more than the Recommendation's 15-bit output, as ffmpeg does,
so every sample of every G.722 file ffmpeg writes equals ffmpeg's, in all
three modes.

### Modes

Modes 2 and 3 give the last one or two bits of each lower sub-band code word
to an auxiliary data channel, leaving 56 or 48 kbit/s of audio. No container
records the mode, so it is chosen with `DecodeOptions(g722Mode = 2)` or `3`;
the default is mode 1, which decodes every bit as audio.

## Seeking

Both codings carry their predictors and scale factors through the whole
stream, with no point at which they restart, and nothing guarantees that two
decodes started in different places come to the same state. So a seek
decodes from the start of the stream, or onward from where the decoder
already is when the target is ahead of it, and lands on exactly the samples
a continuous decode gives, at the cost of decoding everything before the
target. With `DecodeOptions(allowSlowSeek = false)`, a
seek that would have to decode from the start throws
`UnsupportedFeatureException` instead.

## Damaged and unusual files

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| A WAV or AU file stating more than one channel | `UnsupportedFeatureException` |
| A G.726 WAV file whose bits per sample are not 2 to 5 | `CorruptStreamException` |
| A WAV file whose block alignment is not the coding's block size | `CorruptStreamException` |
| A payload that ends early | `TruncatedStreamException`, or in tolerant mode the end of the stream |

Every code word is valid, including the ones an encoder never emits, which
the Recommendations say how to decode, so damage inside the audio decodes to
wrong samples rather than to an error, by AudioKodec or by any decoder.
