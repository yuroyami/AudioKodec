Done. Judgement calls made along the way:

- **Which coefficients.** "The standard downmix coefficients" are those of Recommendation ITU-R BS.775-3, Annex 4, Table 2, for 3/2 material into 1/0, 2/0, 3/0, 2/1, 3/1 and 2/2. I implemented the mix as rules per channel rather than a table per layout pair, and the common tests check that the rules reproduce every row of that table exactly. BS.775 describes a 3/2 system with no low-frequency channel, so the LFE is left out of a mix whose output has none, which is also what ffmpeg does.
- **Matching ffmpeg.** ffmpeg's float mixing (`aformat=channel_layouts=...`) uses those coefficients without normalisation, so a 5.1 to stereo mix gives exactly what ffmpeg gives. I measured ffmpeg's behaviour for the positions BS.775 does not cover rather than reading its source (LGPL), and followed it: side and back pairs stand in for each other, whole when the input has only one of them and at -3 dB for the second when it has both (7.1 into 5.1); a back centre goes to the surrounds at -3 dB each or the fronts at -6 dB each; the front pair either side of centre joins the fronts whole; the top front pair joins the fronts at -3 dB. The oracle suite compares 24 conversions with ffmpeg sample for sample, within float rounding.
- **No normalisation, no clipping.** Like ffmpeg's float path, the mix is not normalised, so a downmix of loud channels can pass full scale. Normalising would make a 5.1 downmix quieter than the same programme's stereo mix, which is the opposite of what a player wants; a caller who needs headroom can apply a gain or `Pcm.clampInPlace`.
- **Upmixing.** Nothing is synthesised: a channel the output has keeps its place at unity and the others stay silent, so stereo into 5.1 fills the front pair alone. Mono into stereo puts the centre on both sides at -3 dB, by the same rule as any centre into a front pair; it is what ffmpeg does and keeps the acoustic power the same, where a copy at unity would be 3 dB louder.
- **What is refused.** A conversion with no defined answer throws `UnsupportedFeatureException` naming the channel, as the issue asked: a layout with channels of unknown position (except into itself, which is the identity), a height channel other than the top front pair (ffmpeg silently drops the top centre, which I did not copy), and a channel with nowhere to go, such as stereo into a layout of only a low-frequency channel.
- **Gain and mid-side.** `Pcm.applyChannelGains` takes one linear gain per channel and works in place. `Pcm.stereoToMidSide` makes mid half the sum and side half the difference, so the pair stays within full scale, and `Pcm.midSideToStereo` undoes it. The round trip is exact for audio of up to 23 bits, whose sums a float holds exactly, which the tests check at 16 and 23 bits, in place and not; beyond that, float rounding applies.
- **API shape.** The issue placed this in `Pcm.kt`, so the four functions sit beside `remapChannels`, with the rules in `internal/dsp/ChannelMixing.kt`. `mixMatrix` returns the coefficients so a caller can see, or reuse, exactly what will be applied.
